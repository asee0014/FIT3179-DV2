Working from home over time

This chart tracks the long-run share of employed people working from home, alongside the lower and upper estimate range observed during the COVID period.

It highlights the jump in remote work during 2020 and the subsequent decline toward more recent patterns.

Most of the time

This chart isolates the "Most of the time" measure across the period in which it is available.

It shows how the share of people working from home most of the time fluctuated before and after the pandemic.

GCCSA working from home

This choropleth shows the share of people working from home across Greater Capital City Statistical Areas.

Areas with darker shading indicate higher proportions of remote work, providing a regional comparison across major city regions.

Lockdowns by state and territory - perhaps can convert to an area cartogram?

This choropleth shows the number of lockdown days and instances across each state and territory.

Areas with darker shading indicate longer periods of lockdown, providing a visual comparison of the impact across different regions.

Net interstate migration

The flexibility of remote work has enabled many Sydney residents to relocate to Queensland seeking better housing affordability, and warmer tropical climates.

Work from home by personal income

This dot plot shows the share of workers who work from home across personal income bands.

It reveals that the highest remote-work incidence is concentrated in higher income groups, while lower income groups remain much less likely to work from home.

Work from home by workplace size

This lollipop chart compares the share of workers who work from home by company size.

Small and very large workplaces stand out as the most remote-work friendly, while mid-sized firms have noticeably lower WFH rates.
