Remote Work in Australia

Working from home (WFH) has become an integral part of daily life for many Australians. 36% of Australians worked from home in 2025.


In this page, we explore current WFH trends, examine the impact from the COVID-19 pandemic, and investigate demographic drivers of remote work. We also take a look at emerging trends including Return to Office mandates future trends with return to office mandates.

Current WFH Stats

Australia's south east has the highest WFH rates. There are significantly higher WFH rates in capital cities compared to regional areas.

The highest WFH rates are found on the east coast of Australia.

CBD workers have higher WFH rates compared to their regional counterparts, with the largest gaps being in Victoria and New South Wales.

The flexibility of remote work has enabled many Sydney residents to relocate to Queensland seeking better housing affordability and climate.

Work from home origins

The COVID-19 pandemic forced many Australian businesses to adopt remote work. After the pandemic these policies remain.

WFH rates had already been trending upwards since the early 2000s. However, government mandated lockdowns during the COVID-19 pandemic caused a significant spike in remote work with upper estimates peaking at 55% of all employed Australians working from home. In the period after the pandemic, WFH rates cooled and settled at their natural rates around 36%.

During the COVID-19 pandemic, state governments had authority over lockdown decisions. Victoria and New South Wales experienced the most days spend in lockdown out of the whole of country (Victoria 302 days, New South Wales 174 days). After the pandemic they retained the highest WFH rates as well.
